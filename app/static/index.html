STATISTICAL METHODOLOGY

How to read the risk review.

The dashboard separates observed losses, conservative bounds, and model-based estimates. Each answers a different question.

Research & system design

Symbolic policy check: Z3 finds no allowed action with positive specified loss in the encoded refund gate. Six removed-guard controls produce counterexamples; 2,000 concrete checks compare the encoding with Python. Exact scope and reproducible query ↗. This is a narrow policy property, not an end-to-end proof of agent safety.

The workflow uses typed, case-bound handoffs and a deterministic payment gate. An optional second model supplies advisory critique. Hash receipts check consistency; they do not authenticate the source or prove the entire system safe.

00 / ADAPTIVE SYSTEMS

Risk while agents adapt

A normal-mixture Hoeffding confidence sequence bounds the running average of conditional expected loss. Losses may depend on previous decisions, agent interactions, and shared memory. The exposure cap and tuning parameter must be fixed before observation.

Each stream receives 2.5% error, giving at least 95% simultaneous coverage across the before/after pair and all observation times within an assessment. This target describes risk over observed episodes; it does not forecast a future workload.

A separate monetary-loss e-process tests whether conditional mean proposed loss exceeds $10 per episode. The frequency and monetary alarms each have a separate 5% false-alert budget; their union is bounded by 10%.

Howard et al. · Annals of Statistics, 2021 ↗
01 / GUARANTEED UNDER ASSUMPTIONS

Time-uniform upper bounds

Four loss thresholds each receive a beta-mixture confidence sequence. Their weighted upper endpoints bound mean loss while allowing repeated observation.

The dashboard allocates 2.5% error to each of the proposed and residual streams. Together, both upper bounds have at least 95% simultaneous coverage within one evaluation under fixed conditional tail probabilities and a $1,000 loss cap.

Construction and assumptions

For each threshold, the mixture likelihood ratio is Eₙ(p) = B(k+½, n−k+½) / [B(½,½) pᵏ(1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ]. Inverting Eₙ(p) < 1/δ gives a confidence sequence. Each stream divides its error budget equally over four thresholds.

Mean upward-binned loss is the weighted sum of tail probabilities. This also upper-bounds exact mean loss. Dependence or changing tail probabilities invalidates this guarantee; adaptive-memory, drift, and correlated suites disable it.

02 / MODEL-BASED ESTIMATE

Frequency × severity

A Beta(0.5, 0.5) frequency prior and a Dirichlet(0.25, 0.25, 0.25, 0.25) severity prior describe mean binned loss. Positive severity bands end at $25, $100, $500, and $1,000.

The 95% credible interval is a posterior statement, not a repeated-sampling guarantee. With sparse data, estimates depend on the prior. Zero observed loss retains nonzero estimated risk.

Posterior and sensitivity

16,000 deterministic posterior draws describe parameter uncertainty about mean binned loss, not the outcome of a future refund. Exported evidence includes a sensitivity range over alternative prior strengths; it is a diagnostic, not a confidence interval.

03 / SEQUENTIAL MONITORING

A mixture of alarms

A single e-process combines six betting rates and precommitted restart times. This broadens sensitivity to moderate changes and later incidents without arbitrary resets.

An alert latches at an e-value of 20. Under a conditional harm probability at or below 10%, the probability of ever crossing is at most 5% per evaluation. This alarm budget is separate from the loss-bound budget.

Mixture and interpretation

Bets are 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, and 5, with equal weights. Half of initial capital starts at case one; the remainder is assigned geometrically to start times 25, 50, 100, and onward. Future components retain their unspent capital.

The alarm tests a reference rate. It does not prove a change point or guarantee detection of every elevated rate. Repeated evaluations do not share one global error budget.

04 / VERIFIED EXECUTION FACTS

Inspect the evidence

Trusted order facts determine authorization. Customer messages and recalled incidents never override the monitor. A read-only policy reviewer records explicit checks and a recomputable evidence fingerprint.

Decision verification recomputes the monitor verdict and review from recorded facts. It detects inconsistencies with those facts; it is not a cryptographic identity check or an end-to-end formal proof.

Evaluation scope

All data and money are synthetic. Offline proposals come from a deliberately naive fixture. Claude evaluations are labeled separately. Protocol-invalid escalations can be blocked without representing prevented monetary loss. Production validation requires representative data, broader harm definitions, and operational controls.
